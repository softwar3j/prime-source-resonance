## Overview

This repository presents an exploratory mathematical note on a **source–resonance interpretation of prime numbers**.

The central intuition is that prime numbers may be viewed not only as isolated arithmetic objects, but also as **source-birth events** in a field of periodic resonances.

In the exact part of the model, divisibility is represented by roots-of-unity resonance kernels. A candidate integer becomes a new source precisely when no relevant earlier source resonates at that site. This reproduces the primes and unique factorization, but it is explicitly acknowledged to be equivalent to classical sieving.

The speculative research question is whether this source-birth picture can be replaced by a natural autonomous operator, field, trace formula, transfer mechanism, or dynamical law that generates the prime source set without an externally imposed scan.

## Core Idea

Traditional number theory treats prime numbers as fundamental discrete objects:

```text
2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ...
```

This note explores a different language:

> A prime is a site where all relevant earlier divisibility waves cancel; once identified, that site becomes a new periodic source.

In this interpretation:

- prime numbers are **newly born sources**,
- composite numbers are **resonance hits**,
- prime powers form **source towers**,
- unique factorization becomes a vector of resonance intensities,
- the Euler product becomes a partition function of independent source towers.

## Mathematical Status

This project does **not** claim:

- a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis,
- a new primality test,
- a new theorem about prime gaps,
- a new proof of the prime number theorem,
- or novelty of the roots-of-unity divisibility filter.

The exact formal component is a harmonic restatement of known arithmetic.

The potential interest lies in the open question:

> Can the local source-birth mechanism be embedded into a non-circular autonomous spectral or dynamical system?

---

## Research Direction

A genuinely new contribution would require at least one of the following:

1. an autonomous generation of the prime source set without candidate-by-candidate sieving;
2. a natural operator whose determinant, spectrum, or trace formula relates to the Euler product;
3. a connection between local divisibility resonances and global zeta-zero oscillations;
4. a derivation of prime density or fluctuations from the dynamics itself;
5. new quantitative information about prime gaps, arithmetic correlations, or zeta zeros;
6. computational leverage over known sieves or primality tests.
